Plan: milestone 11, truck lines on the road

November 14, 2026

Today the console shows each truck as an icon at its last GPS fix, gliding to the next one. Nothing shows the road it takes, and there is no road to show. A simulated truck drives a straight lat/lon line from its home plant to a random site and back, so even a line through its own fixes would cut across fields and the Rhine. M10 left route lines for later.

Milestone 11 puts the simulated trucks on real roads. For each truck that is en route or returning, the map then draws the road of the drive it is on, split at the truck: the road ahead is the main line, and the road already driven is a thinner one. The roads are one committed file of 120 plant-to-site roads, made once from OpenStreetMap with a local OSRM by a new make routes. As each drive begins, the truck reports its road once on a new telematics topic, trucks/{id}/route. The report travels the way every report does, MQTT → ingest → NATS → console → page, on a NATS subject of its own, so a restarted console gets it back. The console decides in Rust which roads show, and the page only draws them.

You chose this line over the road ahead alone and over a trail of GPS fixes (decision 1): it shows where the truck is going and where it has been, it follows the streets, and it comes back after a restart. The other decisions below carry my defaults; tell me where you want something else.

Done when:

  • Simulated trucks drive along OpenStreetMap roads: out to the site and back on the same road, and home along it after a breakdown. Drive times keep today’s model (haversine × 1.3 ÷ 40 km/h, ±10 %), so the existing simulator tests keep their assertions.
  • make routes rebuilds deploy/seed/rhine-neckar.routes.json, with its ODbL notice, from Geofabrik’s extracts and a local OSRM. CI and make test never run it. make test fails when a seed plant or site has no road, or when a road doesn’t fit one route message of 8 KiB.
  • Each truck reports its drive’s road once, as the drive begins, on trucks/{id}/route. Ingest maps it to TruckRouteReported on events.trucks.{id}.route or dead-letters it, dispatch ignores it, and the console keeps it.
  • In the console, every truck that is en route or returning shows its drive’s road, split at its icon, as below. The line goes when the truck arrives or breaks down. A reload, a hidden tab coming back and a restarted console all bring the lines back.
  • No route reaches TravelTimeProvider, the planner or domain::Truck, so the hard constraints (SPEC §4) are untouched. There is no new service, port, variable, metric, stream or consumer.
  • The milestone wraps up:
    • make test is green, make audit is clean, make up is healthy, and make demo works as before (CI’s e2e plays the same scenario).

  • The line: for each truck that is en route or returning, the road of the drive it is on. It runs from where the drive began (its plant, the site, or the spot where it broke down) to where it ends (the site, or its home plant). It is the road as the truck reported it when the drive began: OpenStreetMap’s geometry, simplified to within 5 m, so it follows the streets up to about zoom 17; closer in, a bend can sit up to about 13 px off the road at zoom 18 and 50 px at zoom 20.
  • The split: the road ahead, from the icon on, is the main line, and the road already driven is thinner. The split moves to the icon each time its 1.5 s glide reaches a new fix, or at once under reduced motion, so the driven part never runs ahead of the icon. A hidden tab lets go of the stream, and the snapshot on its return redraws every line. Fixes are 100–180 m apart on most drives, and between them the icon still glides straight. At a sharp corner it leaves the line for up to 1.5 s, by about 11–20 px at zoom 15 and 45–80 px at zoom 17, then sits on it again.
  • When: while the truck is en route or returning. Arrival and a breakdown remove the line, and the next drive brings a new one. Parked trucks have none.
  • Style, one for every line:
    • the truck’s status colour, sky en route and zinc returning, from the existing light and dark tokens;
    • full opacity, which keeps 3.3–4.8:1 against the light basemap and 4.4–8.1:1 against the dark one; at 70 % the light-theme en-route line would drop to about 2.6:1 on a white road;
    • the road ahead 4 px wide and the road driven 2 px, so the two differ by width;
    • dashed on the way home, so en route and returning differ by more than colour (SPEC §9).
    Lines sit under every marker, take no clicks and no focus, and show at every zoom. Below zoom 13 they run into the group badges.
  • Legend: one row across the legend, before “Group of trucks”: “Driven, ahead” beside a sample that is thin, then thick, and “Way home” beside the same sample dashed. Each sample is a 22 px aria-hidden SVG, like the legend’s truck icons. A row of its own keeps both columns as wide as they are, and the first fit depends on the legend’s width. With “Way home” the row is still about 10 px narrower than the two columns, on a desktop and on a phone. The row adds about 24 px of height (22 on a phone). I check the first fit by hand at desktop width and at 390 px in step 7.
  • Words: the popup gains “Heading to Mannheim Kita, Bauwerk AG · 12.4 km to go”, or “Heading home to Weinheim · 3.1 km to go”, hidden when no line shows. The marker’s label adds “, heading to Mannheim Kita, Bauwerk AG” without the km, so it changes once per drive, not with every fix.
  • It is the truck’s own drive, as its telematics report it, not dispatch’s plan. The panel shows the plan, and the two still differ.

The roads

  • The file. deploy/seed/rhine-neckar.routes.json holds a road from each plant to each site: 3 × 40 = 120 roads, keyed by plant id and site id, as [lat, lon] pairs. The way back is the same road reversed. It sits beside the seed, inside the Docker build context (docs/ is outside it), and is compiled into the simulators with include_str!, as the seed is. So there is no runtime file, and no change to compose, Helm, k3d or the Dockerfile. main.rs, the simulators binary, holds the include_str! and passes the text to Roads::parse(&str), and the coverage tests include the file under #[cfg(test)]. The console and the demo link the simulators library for their orders, so the data stays out of the library: only the simulators binary and its image carry the ODbL data, and decision 4’s one label stays right. The console learns roads only from the trucks.
  • Ends. Each road starts exactly at the plant’s seed location and ends exactly at geocoder.locate(site), the postal-code centroid for the 6 address-only sites. The generator adds them before and after OSRM’s snapped ends, so the first and last metres are short straight stubs. The seed has at most 4 decimals, interior points are rounded to 5, and serde_json’s float_roundtrip is on, so the ends compare equal. Trucks leave and arrive exactly where they do today, drive times stay bit-identical for the seeded scenario, and a route’s last point names its plant or site on the page.
  • Size. Douglas-Peucker at 5 m, a constant, keeps about 3–6 points per km: about 11k points and 0.2 MB in all. Full geometry would be about 1.3 MB. The generator never writes a road whose route message would pass 8 KiB, and a test pins the same bound on all 120 committed roads. If a road ever breaks it, the tolerance is the knob.
  • make routes is a one-line target beside sqlx-prepare that runs deploy/seed/routes.sh. The script:
    • starts with set -euo pipefail, goes to the repo root, and checks for docker, curl and osmium with a brew hint, like k8s.sh;
    • downloads Geofabrik’s karlsruhe-regbez, hessen and rheinland-pfalz extracts, 741 MB, into target/routes, because the seed spans all three states;
    • reads each extract’s date with osmium fileinfo -g header.option.osmosis_replication_timestamp, stops if the three differ, and passes the date to the generator;
    • merges them with osmium and clips to the seed’s extent plus about 10 km on each side, a box the generator prints first (cargo run -p simulators --bin routes -- --bounds) from the same Seed and PostalCodeGeocoder. For today’s seed it is -b 8.29,49.15,9.03,49.65, so the A61 near Frankenthal, just west of a 5 km margin, stays inside. The three extracts stay as named. They cover all of today’s box except its south-east corner, in Regierungsbezirk Stuttgart, which no plant-to-site road needs; the generator’s worst-ratio list would show a detour there. A site outside the extracts would show in the same list as a long snap, with its pair named;
    • runs ghcr.io/project-osrm/osrm-backend:v26.11.0-debian, which is multi-arch (there is no plain v26.11.0 tag), with --user "$(id -u):$(id -g)", so target/routes stays yours on Linux. It runs osrm-extract -p /opt/car.lua, osrm-partition and osrm-customize, then osrm-routed --algorithm mld on 127.0.0.1:5005, and stops it on exit;
    • waits up to 60 s until OSRM answers /nearest, so the first request isn’t refused;
    • runs the generator into a temporary file and moves it into place.
  • The generator, crates/simulators/src/bin/routes.rs, takes OSRM’s URL and the extracts’ date, or --bounds alone to print the clip box. It places sites with domain’s Seed and PostalCodeGeocoder, so it can’t drift from the simulators or the console, and talks plain HTTP with reqwest, already a dependency. For each pair it asks /route/v1/driving/{lon},{lat};{lon},{lat}?overview=full&geometries=geojson&exclude=ferry, swaps OSRM’s [lon, lat], adds the exact ends, simplifies and rounds. It writes the notice fields, then one road per line, sorted by plant and site, so a regeneration diffs road by road. It fails on any pair without a road or over the size bound. It prints the count, the size, the largest road and its message size, the largest snap distance and the worst ratio of road to straight line. It lists any pair over 300 m or 2.5, and a few Rhine crossings, each with an https://www.openstreetmap.org/directions?engine=fossgis_osrm_car&route=<lat>,<lon>;<lat>,<lon> link that compares OSM’s own car route with ours. The links hold only public seed coordinates. No image contains the generator, because the Dockerfile builds one --bin per image.
  • Module docs. roads.rs and bin/routes.rs open with a //! doc, as algorithmic modules must (SPEC §15). It says how a point along a road is found, why the straight fallback’s length is haversine × 1.3, and how the generator fixes the ends, simplifies at 5 m, rounds to 5 decimals and keeps each road within one message.
  • Ceiling (ponytail): the pairs are fixed. A new or moved plant or site needs make routes again, and a coverage test fails make test until then. At runtime a missing pair falls back to today’s straight line, reported as a two-point route. Car profile, no traffic, no arbitrary A to B. The upgrade is the same image and clip run as a routing service, ADR 0006’s path.

Trucks on the road

  • A new crates/simulators/src/roads.rs has Road { points, km }, with km cumulative from domain’s Location::distance_km.
    • at(km) finds the segment with partition_point and interpolates inside it. It returns the exact first or last point at or past either end, and guards zero-length spans and roads, so no fix is ever NaN.
    • path(from_km, to_km) is the stretch a drive covers, in its direction: at(from_km), the points strictly between, then at(to_km).
    • Road::straight(from, to) is two points with a length of haversine × 1.3, which reproduces today’s positions and speeds exactly.
    • Roads maps a plant and a site to a road. Its default is empty, which means the straight line everywhere. Roads::parse(&str) parses the file’s text, with an error rather than a panic if a road has fewer than 2 points. main.rs calls it once, with the compiled-in file.
  • Motion::Driving becomes { road, from_km, to_km, duration, dead_zone }. The position is road.at(from_km + (to_km − from_km) × min(elapsed ÷ duration, 1)), constant speed along the road. The reported speed is the distance driven along it, |to_km − from_km|, ÷ the drive time.
  • drive(road, from_km, to_km) takes its ends from road.at() and keeps today’s duration (haversine × 1.3 ÷ 40 km/h × U[0.9, 1.1), at least 60 s), the dead-zone rule and the order of random draws. The road lookup in trip() draws nothing, so seeded runs keep their scenario.
  • TruckSim keeps its current road and its km along it in place of the bare position, starting at Road::straight(home, home) and km 0. The roads table comes through a with_roads() builder like PlantSim::quiet, so TruckSim::new keeps its 7 parameters, clippy’s limit. Out is drive(road, 0, L) and back is drive(road, L, 0). When a drive completes, phase() sets the position and km to its end, so a breakdown that lands before the next phase parks and sets off from the same point.
  • break_down() parks at road.at(km) for the repair, then drives drive(road, km, 0):
    • mid-way out, it turns round and retraces the road to its plant;
    • mid-way back, it carries on home along it;
    • at the site, it takes the whole road reversed, as a normal return does;
    • at the plant, it is today’s zero-length 60 s drive at speed 0.
  • The existing messages keep their topics, cadences, emit(), seq and modem. Their coordinates now lie on roads, and the GPS speed is the distance driven along the road ÷ the drive time. The route message joins them in step 5.

Each drive’s road on the wire

  • The message goes on trucks/{id}/route, with QoS 1 and no retain, through the simulators’ one shared MQTT client and the truck’s own modem, like its other three topics. The body is the vendor header plus the path, [lat, lon] pairs at 5 decimals in the drive’s direction, at least 2 of them. A zero-length drive sends two equal ones. It is text JSON, because the DLQ stores raw payloads as text: 
    {"vehicle":"truck-07","seq":812,"ts_ms":1794236511059,"path":[[49.4875,8.466],…,[49.4786,8.4842]]}
  • When. phase() emits it through emit() for each Driving motion, right after the status (and drum) change and before the first GPS fix: out, back, and home after a repair. So it takes the next seq, and its ts_ms is at or after its status’s, because the simulators’ clock follows tokio’s monotonic one. A dead zone never holds it back: zones start at least 24 s into a drive, and phase() flushes before the status.
  • Size. Every committed road’s route message is 8 KiB or less, measured with the longest truck id and header, so a packet is about 8.2 KB. That is under rumqttc’s 10,240 B default on both the simulators’ and ingest’s clients. A breakdown’s path is a stretch of its road, so it never has more points than the whole road. The default stays, and neither client changes.
  • The event. Event::TruckRouteReported { truck, path: Vec<Location> } is "type": "truck_route_reported" on the wire, with path as {lat, lon} objects like every Location: about 14 KB at most on NATS, whose limit is 1 MB.
    • It goes on a subject of its own, events.trucks.{id}.route. EVENTS already stores events.>, and the console’s and dispatch’s existing events.trucks.> filters already match it.
    • The console’s consumer starts at each subject’s last message. On the truck’s shared subject, the drive’s first GPS fix, sent right after the route, would bury it at once; on its own subject, it is there after any restart.
    • Its id is {vehicle}:{seq}:{ts_ms}, so a modem duplicate falls in JetStream’s 120 s window, and occurred_at is ts_ms.
  • Ingest (telematics.rs): route joins the topic alternation with an explicit arm before the inner _ arm, which means status. Without it, every route would parse as a status and dead-letter with “missing field status”.
    • It parses { path: [[lat, lon], …] }, requires at least 2 points, each finite and in range (latitude ±90, longitude ±180), and maps them to Locations in order.
    • The shared header checks stay: the vehicle matches the topic, and the truck is in the seed.
    • A malformed route takes the existing path: dlq.telematics, the dead-lettered counter and the DLQ gauge. ingest-cli dlq replay --customer telematics maps it once its cause is fixed.
    • Routes stay traced, at about two per truck per trip.
  • Dispatch (intake.rs): the variant joins the span’s truck pattern and the => None arm beside drum rotation and off-plan tickets, with a comment that routes are for the map. Its fleet consumer filters events.trucks.>, so it acks each route and does nothing. The module doc’s events.trucks.* becomes events.trucks.>. So do the README’s and architecture.md’s, because * doesn’t match a route’s four-token subject. ADR 0001 becomes “Accepted, amended” and gets one paragraph, as 0005 records its amendment: “Amendment (milestone 11): each truck’s route has a subject of its own, events.trucks.<id>.route, so the trucks’ subjects are events.trucks.>.”

The console’s rule

  • State keeps each truck’s newest route beside its TruckView, not inside it: a path and its time, newest by occurred_at. TruckView doesn’t grow, so a GPS push stays about 194 B and never carries a path.
  • One rule, in Rust: a route shows if and only if its truck’s status is en_route or returning, and the route’s time is at or after the status’s. At or after, not the strict newer(), because a route can carry its status’s millisecond. Every later status has a later time, so it hides the line.
  • The console runs the rule after every accepted route, status and /world merge, under the same lock as the change. apply() makes a status change and the route change it causes, and sends both, before it releases the lock, as load_world does for its batch. That keeps subscribe()’s promise: a change is in the snapshot or among the changes, never in both. It remembers what it last pushed for each truck, and pushes a route event only when what shows changes: {"truck":"truck-07","path":[…]}, or "path":null.
  • snapshot.routes holds only the routes that show, keyed by truck id, and is worked out when the snapshot is taken.
  • The existing pushed{kind} counter and event_lag{kind} histogram count route pushes, so there is no new metric, and the dashboard’s by-kind panels show them without an edit. The lag leaves out events received before the console started, so the startup replay of each truck’s last route doesn’t skew it.

The page’s lines

  • Drawing. One setRoute(t, path) takes both the route events and the snapshot’s routes. It removes the truck’s two lines, and for a path it draws two polylines in the overlay pane, under every marker:
    • className route driven s-<status> and route ahead s-<status>;
    • interactive: false;
    • a weight equal to the CSS width, because Leaflet clips by it.
    The class is set once. A status change that alters what shows always brings a push, and a truck’s status reaches the page before the route it shows.
  • After replace(), applySnapshot calls setRoute(t, s.routes[id] ?? null) for every truck, so trucks that addTruck created get their lines, and a line whose truck arrived or broke down while the stream was closed goes too. replace() removes a vanished truck’s lines with its marker. All of it sits behind the existing map guard. The overlay pane is marked aria-hidden, like the page’s other decorative SVGs, because the words carry the meaning.
  • The split happens when a glide ends: in frame() at f === 1, right after glides.delete(t), and in glide()’s jump branch.
    • The page projects the truck’s fix with L.Projection.SphericalMercator. It searches the route’s segments, projected once when the route arrived, from the previous split onwards with the public L.LineUtil.closestPointOnSegment, and keeps the first of the nearest.
    • The road driven runs up to that point, and the road ahead runs from it.
    • A new route starts the search at its first segment, and splits at once unless the icon is gliding.
    • Not Polyline.closestLayerPoint: it scans only the clipped, on-screen parts.
    • Ponytail: a straight scan of at most about 430 segments per fix, fine for 25 trucks.
  • Kilometres to go: the length of the road ahead, summed with map.distance and shown with num().
  • Words. The place is the route’s last point looked up in markedSpots, which holds plants by name and sites as “name, customer”, because site names repeat across customers. The console places sites with geocoder.locate, as the roads’ ends do, and every seed coordinate has at most 4 decimals, so the 5-decimal spot() key matches. The row says “Heading home to” when that point is the truck’s home plant, and “Heading to” otherwise. With no match it shows only the km. truckPopup gains the row, filled by refresh(), which already runs every second. paintTruck adds “, heading to <place>” to the label.
  • CSS: .route takes stroke: var(--status, var(--text-muted)), no fill, round caps and joins, and no opacity. .route.ahead is 4 px and .route.driven 2 px, and .route.s-returning adds the dash. The legend samples reuse these classes: the “Driven, ahead” sample has no status and takes the neutral --text-muted, and the “Way home” sample carries s-returning for its dash, whose zinc matches --text-muted in both themes. Leaflet’s SVG renderer uses presentation attributes and CSSOM transforms, which the CSP allows, so its test passes unchanged.
  • A ponytail comment by the line code says the lines need attribution: ATTRIBUTION if the basemap ever stops being based on OpenStreetMap.
  • The page has no rule of its own: the console sends only routes that show and pushes every change.

Restarts, reorders and breakdowns

A line can be missing, never wrong.

  • Console restart: the state starts from the seed. The consumer starts at each subject’s last message, which now includes each truck’s last route on events.trucks.{id}.route, however many fixes followed it. /world brings each status. Whichever lands first, the rule runs again when the other lands, so a driving truck’s line is back within seconds. It is as fresh as /world’s status, a limit truck statuses already have. A parked truck’s last route is older than its status, so it stays hidden.
  • Stale replays: a running console ignores a route replayed from the DLQ when it isn’t newer than the one it holds. The consumer takes each subject’s last message by stream order, not by time, so after a console restart the replayed route can be the one it starts from. It is older than the truck’s current status, so the rule hides it, and that truck’s line waits for its next drive.
  • NATS reconnect: the console keeps its memory, the consumer starts again at each subject’s last message, and a replayed route that isn’t newer changes nothing. /world is read again, and its merge reruns the rule, so a truck that arrived during the outage loses its line.
  • Page reload, hidden tab: the next snapshot brings back every line that shows and takes away every other.
  • Simulators restart: every truck reports available first, so every line goes, and each next drive brings a new one.
  • Duplicates and reorders: JetStream drops a repeat by its id, and one that slips through isn’t newer. The modem holds a message until its next send outside a dead zone. At a drive’s start no dead zone is open, so a drive’s status, drum and route each swap at most with the message after them. A route that lands before its status is kept and shows when the status lands. One that lands after the first fix shows all the same.
  • Loss: a route lost on the simulators’ clean-session reconnect, or dead-lettered, leaves that drive without a line. The previous drive’s route stays hidden, because it is older than the new status.
  • Dead zones: buffered fixes flush in order. The icon glides straight across the gap, and the split catches up when its last glide ends.
  • Breakdowns: out_of_service hides the line. After the repair, returning and its route show the way home from the breakdown point, along the same road. A breakdown at the plant reports two identical points, which draw at most a dot under the plant’s marker.
  • Make chaos cuts only the plant-agent’s uplink. Truck telematics go straight to the cloud’s Mosquitto, so lines carry on.

The OpenStreetMap data

  • I read the roads file as a substantial Derivative Database of OpenStreetMap under ODbL 1.0: coordinates for software to read, taken systematically from far more than 100 ways. That is a conservative reading, not legal advice (decision 4).
  • So the file is offered under ODbL on its own. It is keyed by our plant and site ids and kept apart from the seed, so the seed and the code stay MIT, as the OSMF’s Collective Database guideline allows.
  • Its notice travels with it. attribution “© OpenStreetMap contributors”, license “ODbL-1.0”, license_url and source (OSRM’s version and profile, the extracts’ date, the 5 m tolerance) sit inside the file, and the coverage test checks that they are there.
  • README’s License section gets one line, linking the licence, as ODbL §4.2 asks, and OpenStreetMap’s copyright page: “[deploy/seed/rhine-neckar.routes.json](deploy/seed/rhine-neckar.routes.json) contains information from [OpenStreetMap](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright), which is made available here under the [Open Database License (ODbL)](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1-0/). © OpenStreetMap contributors.” It lands in the same commit as the file.
  • The file is compiled into the simulators binary, so the image CI pushes to GHCR carries it too. CI’s docker/metadata-action step labels every image with the repository’s licence, org.opencontainers.image.licenses=MIT, so the simulators image would claim MIT while it carries ODbL data. By default I correct that with one line on that step (decision 4). The action’s custom labels replace generated ones of the same name, while a Dockerfile LABEL would lose to the generated one. I check the label in the pushed image’s config after the next push to main.
  • ODbL §4.6 asks for the method to be available: the file is public, and routes.sh and bin/routes.rs are the method.
  • The route messages and events carry stretches of these roads through our own broker and stream. The drawn lines are a Produced Work. The map’s existing “© OpenStreetMap” credit, linked to openstreetmap.org/copyright, covers them.
  • OSRM, BSD-2-Clause, runs only on your machine, and nothing of it ships.

Security

  • The running stack gains one MQTT topic and one NATS subject, and no port, service, stream or consumer. The console serves the lines from its own state over its existing stream, so the CSP and its test stay as they are. The page still loads only its own files, Leaflet and the tiles.
  • Ingest checks every route at the trust boundary: the shared header (the vehicle matches the topic, and the truck is in the seed), and at least two points, each finite and in range. A malformed route goes to the DLQ, never to the map, and no route reaches TravelTimeProvider, the planner or domain::Truck.
  • A message over rumqttc’s 10,240 B limit from any other sender would stall ingest’s MQTT intake instead of dead-lettering. That is how every truck topic behaves today, and the 8 KiB bound keeps our simulators well clear of it.
  • The console holds at most one route per seed truck, 25 in all. Our simulators’ routes have at most about 430 points; another sender’s are bounded only by rumqttc’s 10,240 B packet, about 1,700 short points.
  • make routes runs by hand. It downloads Geofabrik’s extracts over HTTPS, pulls an OSRM image pinned to an exact tag, binds OSRM to 127.0.0.1:5005 only, and stops it on exit. The generator talks only to that OSRM, parses its answers with serde, and writes a temporary file that replaces the old one only when every road is there and fits.
  • The roads file is data compiled into a binary, never code. One road per line keeps its diff readable, and I read it before I commit it.
  • Nothing personal enters the repo: public OpenStreetMap roads, keyed by our simulated seed.
